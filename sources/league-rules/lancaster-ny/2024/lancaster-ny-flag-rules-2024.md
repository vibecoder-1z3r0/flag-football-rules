# Lancaster NY Flag Football Rules (2024)

> All rulings below supersede the official USA Flag Football rules (or are re-iterated for
> emphasis). If a rule is not illustrated here, refer to the official USA Football Flag Rulebook
> (see `base-rules/usa-football-flag-rulebook.md`) or the condensed NFL FLAG field rules.

Officials are teachers and the adjudicators, judges and arbiters of the game while it is being
played. The authority of the game is delegated to and is the responsibility of the officials. It is
not a matter of "command and control" but ensuring that both sides play a fair game of Flag
Football within the rules set forth. Penalty enforcement is a part of the game, however teaching
and warning is also acceptable based on the officials' judgment, with a declaration of "next time
it will be enforced". Penalties should move to straight enforcement as the season progresses.

If there are two officials on the field, they both work as a team to achieve the above outcomes,
playing distinct and equal roles on the field of play, helping each other and accepting help from
each other.

## Quick Facts

- 4 downs (see below for differences and clarifications)
- 25 minute halves, running clock
- 2 timeouts per team per half (30 seconds each)
- Mouth guards are required for play.
- Flags will be used by the officials to mark penalties (yellow) and changes of possession (blue)
  on interceptions (blue flags are not required). The flag shall be thrown in the general
  direction near the spot of the infraction or change of possession.

## 4 Downs

**Peewee** — offense has 4 downs to get to the first-down line to gain, then another 4 downs to
get to the goal line. If the offense does not get to the line to gain or goal line, possession
changes and the other team starts at its own 5-yard line. Interceptions are still a change of
possession at the end of the intercepting team's forward progress (except on PATs — the play
is over if a PAT is intercepted).

**FR and JV** — offense has 4 downs to get to the first-down line to gain, and another set to the
goal line. On 4th down, the coach must decide, prior to the snap, whether they are going for it
or "punting". If they elect to punt, the ball is placed at the opposing team's 5-yard line and
possession changes. If they do not elect to punt and fail to get to the line to gain, the ball
changes possession at the end of forward progress. If this is within 5 yards of the target end
zone and the offense fails to score, the ball is placed at the 5-yard line and possession
changes. (Any change of possession within the 5-yard line of the defending team's end zone
results in the ball being placed at the 5-yard line.)

## 25 Minute Halves, Running Clock

Two 25-minute halves (running clock) with a 2-minute halftime.

### Starting and Stopping the Game Clock

- If a timeout is called, the clock stops and restarts at the snap of the next "timed down play"
  (PATs are not timed downs). Same for any other clock stoppage or the start of each half.
- The clock is not stopped during enforcement of a penalty (unless a timeout was called).
- The clock may be stopped by an official at their discretion.

### Timeouts

Each team gets 2 timeouts per half, 30 seconds each. All timeouts are called by coaches. A
timeout may be used by a coach to ask the official for a ruling, clarification, or question about a
ruling. If the clarification/ruling favors the coach (or it was a legitimate question), the ref can
award the timeout back.

If a team calls a timeout with none remaining, a delay-of-game penalty is enforced. If such a
timeout in error is called by the offensive team with the game clock under 2:00 remaining, a
30-second runoff is enforced in addition to the delay-of-game penalty.

## 2 Puck System

A 2-puck system is used: one puck marks the line of scrimmage, the other marks the rush line.

## Rushing (JV / Freshman / Peewee differences)

7 yards back for JV; 10 yards back for Freshmen/Peewee.

## Snapping

The ball must be snapped from on top of the puck marking the line of scrimmage. If no puck is
present, the ball must be snapped from a position where the ball contacts the ground. Failure to
do so results in a failed-snap penalty.

## Failed Snaps (JV / Freshman / Peewee differences)

Freshman and Peewee: 2 failed snaps allowed per team per half. The official reminds the
offending coach how many failed snaps they've used and how many remain, or rules a loss of
down once they're out of "failed snaps".

JV: any failed snap results in a loss of down.

## Flag Belt

Prior to the game, all players' flag belts should be checked for legal flag positioning and
maintenance of the "third flag" / belt slack. Flag positioning is on the front side of the hip. The
third flag (belt slack) should be secured so it isn't confused with one of the other valid flags.

## No Laterals, No Screen Passes

The ball must be handed off player to player. If there is a pass, the ball must travel past the line
of scrimmage or it is an "illegal forward pass" and the appropriate penalty is called.

## Determining Forward Progress

The ball NEVER determines forward progress.

- When the flag is pulled, the runner's frontmost foot determines forward progress.
- If the player is down by any part of the body touching the ground besides feet or hands, the
  furthest "downed body part" (including feet, but not hands) determines forward progress.

### Illegal Forward Pass Clarification

If any part of the quarterback's foot (or a player who received a handoff) goes over the line of
scrimmage during a passing play, an illegal forward pass penalty should be assessed.

## Starting the Play / Snapping

A reasonable amount of time should be allotted for the defense to line up — a judgment call, and
the official should be vocal in directing the offense to delay if needed. If the play starts from a
clock stoppage (or the beginning of each half), the official directs the offense to wait to start
their cadence until the "return to play" whistle has blown.

The ball must start on the ground (or puck) where it was spotted, and the snap must be
delivered through the center's legs.

## Last Play of the Game

The clock is a running clock and the game ends when time expires. Teams get 2 timeouts per
half and can use them to stop the clock at any point (not in the middle of a play).

## Spotting the Ball and the Play Clock

The 30-second play clock starts when the ball is spotted; the official is then responsible for
marking off the rush line. A verbal reminder/countdown should be given around 20 seconds left.
A team gets a warning for the first delay-of-game violation, then a delay-of-game penalty from
there on. If this happens with under 2 minutes to play, the clock is stopped (as if from a
timeout).

## Rules Debate

If a rules debate needs to occur, it happens at midfield with the ref and both head coaches,
along with someone from the Lancaster Flag Administration (John, Tom, Andrew, Bryon, Jeff) if
available.

## Official Positioning and Systems

The official maintains the rush line, in line with where the ball is spotted, focusing attention on
where the ball is (or is heading, for a pass) while maintaining as large a field of view as
possible.

If there is a second official (line judge), they maintain the line of scrimmage on one of the
sidelines. In a two-official system, the rush-line official generally "floats" to the opposite sideline
to view out-of-bounds calls.

The primary official is the one at the rush line (two-official system); however both officials can
call penalties and stop play. The line judge maintains the game clock and reminds their partner
and coaches how much time is left at any change of possession and as the clock nears zero
(frequent reminders under 10 minutes are OK). The primary official audibly communicates any
called penalties, handles communications with coaches and captains, and handles the coin toss
and calling teams back to play for the 2nd half.
