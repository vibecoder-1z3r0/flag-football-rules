# Casebook

Illustrative case plays, in NCAA/NFHS casebook style: **Play** → **Ruling** → **Reference**. These
exist to resolve edge cases that the rule text alone doesn't make obvious. Each entry is
cross-referenced from the rule it illustrates in `rulebook.md`.

---

### CP-1 — Starting a Play After a Clock Stoppage

**Play:** A timeout ends. The offense immediately breaks the huddle and starts its cadence
before the official has signaled play should resume.

**Ruling:** Illegal — the offense must wait for the official's "return to play" whistle before
starting its cadence after any clock stoppage (timeout, halftime, injury, etc.). The official should
proactively direct the offense to hold if they start too soon, rather than only penalizing after the
fact.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §3.3; `officials-manual.md` §OM-3.

---

### CP-2 — Failure to Convert Inside the No-Run Zone

**Play:** FR division. It's 4th down, offense is on the defense's 3-yard line (inside the 5-yard
no-run zone before the end zone). The coach elects to go for it rather than punt. The play fails to
reach the goal line.

**Ruling:** Because the spot of the failed conversion (the 3-yard line) falls within the no-run
zone nearest the defending team's end zone, the ball is placed at the 5-yard line — not left at
the 3-yard line — before possession changes to the defense.

**Contrast:** If the same failed 4th-down attempt happens outside any no-run zone (say, the
defense's 20-yard line), the defense simply takes over at that exact spot with no adjustment.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §5.2, §5.4.

---

### CP-3 — Interception Returned on a Conversion Attempt

**Play:** After a touchdown, the offense attempts a 2-point conversion from the 10-yard line. The
defense intercepts the pass at the goal line and returns it the length of the field.

**Ruling:** Under current (2026) rules, this is a live, returnable play — if the defender reaches
the opposite end zone, the defense scores 2 points. This is a deliberate departure from the USA
Football baseline, under which the play would be ruled dead the instant of the interception with
no return.

**Officials Notes:** Don't blow the whistle at the interception — play continues to a normal dead-
ball result (out of bounds, down, or score) exactly as on a regular-play interception.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §4.2, §9.3.

---

### CP-4 — Bad Snap / Botched Exchange

**Play:** The snap sails over the quarterback's head and hits the ground 3 yards behind the
original line of scrimmage before anyone recovers it. Peewee division, first such failure of the
half.

**Ruling:** The play is dead the instant the ball hits the ground — no live fumble recovery.
Because this is a Peewee-division snap/exchange failure and it's the team's first of the half, it's
a do-over: replay the down from the original line of scrimmage, no loss of down or yardage.
(The ball would otherwise be placed where it hit the ground, per the general dead-ball rule, but
the snap/exchange-failure do-over allowance takes precedence for the first two such failures
per half in Peewee/Freshman/Girls Freshman.)

**Contrast:** The same play in JV/Varsity, or a 3rd+ such failure in Peewee/Freshman/Girls
Freshman in the same half, is a loss of down (no yardage lost) rather than a do-over. If it
happens in the offense's own end zone in JV/Varsity, it's a safety.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §6.2, §7.5, §11.2, §4.3.

### CP-5 — Flag Guarding After Already Gaining the First Down

**Play:** JV division. The ball carrier crosses the line-to-gain by 5 yards (first down easily
gained) but then, still on the same play, stiff-arms a defender reaching for the flags 5 yards past
the line-to-gain.

**Ruling:** This is an edge case explicitly addressed in the 2026 source rules. The first down is
still awarded — the offense's drive continues and that team cannot gain another first down on
the current drive (i.e., if the no-run zone before the end zone is reached, it behaves as the last
zone of the drive). The flag-guarding penalty yardage is assessed from the spot of the foul on
top of that. The next legal play is "first down and goal" (or "first down," if not yet inside the
no-run zone before the end zone).

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §7.4; `penalty-matrix.md` (Flag Guarding).

### CP-6 — Illegal Forward Pass via Passer's Foot Crossing the LOS

**Play:** The quarterback, under pressure, steps up and releases a pass with their plant foot
landing a yard beyond the original line of scrimmage. The pass is complete 10 yards downfield.

**Ruling:** Illegal forward pass. Any part of the passer's foot crossing the line of scrimmage
during a passing play makes the pass illegal, regardless of whether it's completed. This also
applies to a player who received a handoff and then throws — their foot must stay behind the
LOS at release.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §8.4.

### CP-7 — Rusher Resetting After Leaving the Rush Line Early

**Play:** JV division. A rusher breaks from the 7-yard rush line before the snap, then realizes
their mistake and retreats back behind the puck before the snap occurs.

**Ruling:** Legal — a rusher who leaves the rush line early may return to it, reset, and then
legally rush once the ball is snapped. No penalty, since no illegal-rush infraction is complete
unless the rusher actually crosses the line of scrimmage in pursuit of the quarterback before a
handoff or pass.

**Contrast:** If that same rusher crosses the line of scrimmage before the snap (rather than
just leaving the rush line), that's offside, not illegal rush — see `penalty-matrix.md`.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §10.3.

---

### CP-8 — Overtime Shootout Progression

**Play:** Game ends regulation tied. The visiting team (which called the opening coin toss) also
calls the overtime coin toss and wins it, electing to attempt first. Officials pick an end zone and
explain the format to both teams and the fans there. Round 1: Team A (visiting) converts its
1-point attempt from the 5-yard line; Team B does not.

**Ruling:** Team A wins immediately — no further rounds are played, since one team succeeded
and the other did not.

**Contrast:** If both teams convert (or both fail) in Round 1, both move to the 10-yard line for
Round 2, and the same succeed/fail logic applies each round until exactly one team converts and
the other doesn't. Team A stays "first" and Team B stays "second" for every round — the
first/second order decided at the single overtime coin toss is not re-decided round to round.

**Reference:** `rulebook.md` §1.1, §13.1. Applies to regular-season games and playoffs alike.
